Beneficial ownership in Kenya: what your company must keep and file

Your share register shows who owns shares. The beneficial ownership register shows who really owns and controls the company. Kenyan law asks you to keep both.

LexReg Legal Team

2 Oct 2026 · 4 min read

Under the Companies Act 2015 and its beneficial ownership regulations, every company must identify the people who ultimately own or control it, record them in a register, and lodge that register with the Registrar of Companies.

Who counts as a beneficial owner

A beneficial owner is a natural person who meets at least one of four tests, directly or through other entities:

10%or more of the issued shares
10%or more of the voting rights
A right to appoint or remove a director
Significant influence or control over the company

When a shareholder is itself a company, look through it to the people behind it. Their indirect stake counts.

Example

James Kariuki Savannah Holdings Limited Amani Logistics Limited

Savannah Holdings Limited holds 100 of Amani's 1,000 shares (10%). Savannah cannot be a beneficial owner, so Amani records James Kariuki, who controls Savannah, as an indirect beneficial owner.

What you must keep

The register sits with the company and records, for each beneficial owner:

  • Full name, ID or passport number, KRA PIN, nationality and date of birth
  • Postal, residential and email addresses, and a phone number
  • How they own or control the company, and whether directly or indirectly
  • The date they became, or stopped being, a beneficial owner

What you must file

Lodge a copy of the register with the Registrar within 30 days of preparing it, on form BOF-1 through eCitizen. When anything changes, such as a share transfer, a new right to appoint directors or a person who no longer qualifies, update the register and lodge the change too.

An outdated register is an offence. Failing to keep or lodge it exposes the company and every officer in default to penalties.
